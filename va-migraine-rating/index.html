VA rating for migraines: what each level requires

VA rates migraine at 0, 10, 30 or 50 percent. The whole rule is four lines long, and the difference between levels comes down to how often you have a certain kind of attack. This page goes through the four lines one at a time.

An independent explainer, not from the VA and not legal advice. I'm Fiona, the maker of this log; I am not a veteran, a lawyer or an accredited representative. For help with a claim, accredited Veterans Service Organization representatives help for free. Find one on VA.gov.

The four lines

Migraine is rated under 38 CFR § 4.124a, Diagnostic Code 8100. This is the published wording:

RatingThe regulation says
50%With very frequent completely prostrating and prolonged attacks productive of severe economic inadaptability
30%With characteristic prostrating attacks occurring on an average once a month over last several months
10%With characteristic prostrating attacks averaging one in 2 months over last several months
0%With less frequent attacks

Fifty percent is the highest rating this diagnostic code provides. Separate rules, extraschedular consideration and total disability based on individual unemployability (TDIU), can in some circumstances apply beyond the schedule. They are not covered here; an accredited representative can explain them.

What moves you from one level to the next

Read down the table and one thing changes from line to line: how often prostrating attacks happen, averaged over the last several months.

  • 10%: about one prostrating attack every two months.
  • 30%: about one a month.
  • 50%: "very frequent". The regulation gives no number. VA's manual for its raters, the M21-1, puts it this way: "Characteristic prostrating attacks, on average, are less than one month apart over the last several months." That is guidance for VA's raters and does not bind the Board of Veterans' Appeals.

The 50% line also adds three conditions the lower lines don't have: the attacks are completely prostrating, they are prolonged, and they are productive of severe economic inadaptability.

The frequency counted is prostrating attacks, which is narrower than migraine days or headache days. VA must still consider all of your migraine symptoms when it decides.

The phrases, one at a time

"Prostrating" and "completely prostrating"

The regulation doesn't define either. The M21-1 describes prostrating as "causing extreme exhaustion, powerlessness, debilitation or incapacitation with substantial inability to engage in ordinary activities," and completely prostrating as "extreme exhaustion or powerlessness with essentially total inability to engage in ordinary activities." There is more on this, and on what to write down, on the page about the word "prostrating".

"On an average … over last several months"

The rule looks at an average across months. One bad month, or one good one, is not the whole picture. The regulation doesn't say how many months "several" is. That is one reason a log kept over several months can be useful.

"Productive of severe economic inadaptability"

This is about work. Board of Veterans' Appeals decisions, citing Pierce v. Principi, 18 Vet. App. 440 (2004), read "productive of" as either producing or capable of producing that result. The M21-1 says it does not mean a person is incapable of any substantially gainful employment. Evidence people commonly point to includes sick leave used and unpaid absences.

"Less frequent attacks"

The 0% line. A 0% rating still means the condition is service-connected; it is rated as non-compensable (38 CFR § 4.31).

What a log can and can't do

A log can show, month by month, how many attack days you had, on how many of them you had to stop and lie down, how long they lasted, and how many cost you work. Those are the things the four lines ask about.

A log can't decide your rating, and this page can't tell you which rating you would get. A log is one piece of evidence among others; it doesn't replace a diagnosis or a medical opinion. VA decides, usually with a C&P examiner's report (what the C&P exam asks). VA's manual (M21-1, V.iii.12.A.3, change date September 14, 2023) says a person's own account can establish prostration if it is credible and medical evidence ties the symptoms to migraine, and that headache journals may be accepted as credible lay testimony.

So keep it honest and keep it going. Log the bad days in your own words, log the ordinary days too, and write each entry on the day it happens if you can.
