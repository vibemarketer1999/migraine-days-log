Prior authorization for a migraine preventive: what insurers ask

Prior authorization for a newer migraine preventive keeps coming back to one count: migraine days per month. Three of the four policies below want at least 4 a month to start, and renewal asks to see a benefit, usually fewer migraine days. Here is what they say about Aimovig, Qulipta, Nurtec ODT and Vyepti.

An independent explainer, not medical advice. I'm Fiona, the maker of this log; I'm not a doctor and I don't work for an insurer. Your own plan's policy is the one that counts, and your doctor's office usually files the request.

Four published policies

Cigna, Aimovig (effective 1 June 2026)

To start: 18 or older, and "≥ 4 migraine headache days per month (prior to initiating a migraine-preventive medication)". Approved for 1 year.

To renew: The same two conditions, plus "significant clinical benefit from the medication, as determined by the prescriber", for example fewer migraine days a month. Another year.

Express Scripts, Qulipta (reviewed 26 Feb 2025)

To start: The same two conditions: 18 or older, at least 4 migraine headache days a month before any preventive. Approved for 1 year.

To renew: The same "significant clinical benefit" wording.

Aetna, Nurtec ODT and Qulipta for prevention (policy 3481-E, June 2024 version)

To start: An adult. The pharmacy first checks your fill history for an older preventive (or, for Nurtec ODT, for two triptans); if it doesn't find one, the claim goes to prior authorization. New starts are approved for 3 months.

To renew: At least 3 months of treatment, and "a reduction in migraine days per month from baseline". Renewal lasts 12 months.

UnitedHealthcare, Vyepti (commercial) (effective 1 Sept 2026)

To start: A migraine diagnosis; 4 to 7 migraine days a month (with fewer than 15 headache days, or the provider attests migraine is the main cause) or 8 or more. Plus "trial and failure (after a trial of at least two months), contraindication, or intolerance" of two older preventives, such as a beta-blocker, topiramate or amitriptyline (Botox counts after two quarterly rounds). And the same for two of Aimovig, Emgality, Nurtec ODT or Qulipta, after at least three months each.

To renew: "A positive response to therapy, demonstrated by a reduction in headache frequency and/or intensity".

Cigna, Express Scripts and UnitedHealthcare also rule out taking two CGRP preventives at the same time. None of the four names a particular kind of record, like a diary. They ask for the count, and the count has to come from somewhere.

The baseline is the number to get right

Note the words "prior to initiating a migraine-preventive medication". The count the plan wants is from before treatment started, and none of these policies says how it has to be documented. If you're already on an older preventive, ask your doctor's office which period the plan will accept.

Renewal works the other way round. Aetna and UnitedHealthcare both ask for a reduction: fewer migraine days than at the start, or fewer or milder headaches. Showing that is easiest with the same count, kept the same way, for the months after.

So if a new preventive is a possibility, months logged now are the "before" you'll want later.

Step therapy: the other thing to keep

Many plans make you try cheaper preventives first. UnitedHealthcare's Vyepti policy wants two older ones, each for at least two months unless you couldn't take it, and then two of the newer drugs. Aetna's pharmacy checks your fill history for an older preventive before Nurtec ODT or Qulipta. Cigna's Aimovig policy and Express Scripts' Qulipta policy list no such step. Express Scripts sums up the American Headache Society's 2024 position as CGRP-targeting drugs being "a first-line approach … without a requirement for prior failure of other classes of migraine preventive treatment".

Plans differ, so keep the list either way: each preventive you've tried, roughly when you started and stopped, and why you stopped (it didn't help, side effects, couldn't take it). Your doctor's office may be asked for it.
